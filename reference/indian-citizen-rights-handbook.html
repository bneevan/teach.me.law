Indian Citizen Legal Rights Field Handbook

A concise, practical quick-reference guide covering arrest rights, FIR procedures, cheque bounce timelines, and consumer remedies under landmark Supreme Court precedents.

1. The Police Arrest & Detention Shield (D.K. Basu Guidelines)

Established by the Supreme Court in D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal (1997 1 SCC 416), these 11 guidelines are mandatory for every police officer in India. Failure to comply makes the officer liable for contempt of court and departmental action.

  • 1. Visible Identification Badges Police personnel carrying out arrest and interrogation must wear clear identification and name tags with accurate designations. Mandatory D.K. Basu Rule 1
  • 2. Mandatory Memo of Arrest at the Scene The arresting officer must prepare a written "Memo of Arrest" at the time of arrest. It must state the exact time and date of arrest and must be countersigned by at least one witness (a family member or respected member of the locality) and the arrestee. Section 41B CrPC / Sec 36 BNSS
  • 3. Right to Inform Family / Friends Within 8–12 Hours The arrestee has an absolute right to have one friend, relative, or person interested in their welfare notified of the arrest and the exact place of custody within 8 to 12 hours. Section 41A/41B CrPC
  • 4. Independent Medical Examination Every 48 Hours The arrested person must be medically examined by a trained government doctor at the time of arrest and every 48 hours during police custody. Any major or minor injuries existing on the body must be recorded in an "Inspection Memo" signed by both the doctor and arrestee. Section 54 CrPC / Sec 53 BNSS
  • 5. Protection for Women & Sunset Rules Under Section 46(4) of CrPC (Sec 43(5) BNSS), no woman can be arrested after sunset and before sunrise, except in exceptional circumstances where prior written permission has been obtained from a Judicial Magistrate First Class by a female police officer. Section 46(4) CrPC
  • 6. Production Before Magistrate Within 24 Hours Under Article 22(2) of the Constitution and Section 57 CrPC, an arrested individual cannot be detained in police custody beyond 24 hours without being presented before the nearest Judicial Magistrate. Constitution of India Art 22(2)

2. FIR & Zero FIR Action Protocol

Under Lalita Kumari v. Govt of UP (2014), registering an FIR is non-negotiable whenever information reveals a cognizable crime.

  • Zero FIR Right: If a crime occurs outside the station's territory, police CANNOT turn you away. They must register a Zero FIR (serial number "0"), start preliminary medical or investigative aid, and transfer the case to the jurisdictional police station.
  • Free Copy of the FIR: Under Section 154(2) CrPC, a copy of the registered FIR must be handed over to the informant or complainant immediately, free of charge. Never leave the police station without your signed and stamped FIR copy.
  • What if the Police Refuse to Register an FIR?
    1. Send a written complaint via Registered Post with Acknowledgment Due to the Superintendent of Police (SP) or Deputy Commissioner of Police (DCP) under Section 154(3) CrPC.
    2. If the SP fails to act within a reasonable time, file an application before the Judicial Magistrate First Class under Section 156(3) CrPC. The Magistrate possesses statutory power to order the police station to register an FIR and submit an investigation report.

3. Cheque Bounce Recovery Protocol (Section 138 NI Act)

To convert an unpaid civil debt into a quasi-criminal remedy carrying up to 2 years imprisonment and double the cheque penalty, you must strictly follow this statutory timeline:

Step Statutory Trigger Mandatory Deadline
1. Present Cheque Present cheque to bank within validity Within 3 months from cheque date
2. Receive Memo Bank issues Return Memo ("Insufficient Funds") Day 0 (Clock Starts)
3. Send Legal Notice Send statutory demand notice via Registered Post Within 30 Days of receiving Bank Return Memo
4. 15-Day Grace Period Drawer given statutory window to pay 15 Days from notice receipt
5. File Complaint File complaint before Judicial Magistrate (JMFC/CMM) Within 30 Days after the 15-day grace period expires

4. Consumer Redressal & Free Legal Aid (NALSA)

  • e-Daakhil Online Filing (CPA 2019): File consumer complaints online against defective products, airline cancellations, builder delays, and deceptive ads without hiring an advocate at edaakhil.nic.in.
  • Pecuniary Jurisdictions (CPA 2019): District Commission: claims up to ₹50 Lakhs; State Commission: claims from ₹50 Lakhs to ₹2 Crores; National Commission (NCDRC): claims exceeding ₹2 Crores.
  • Free Legal Aid (Article 39A / NALSA): Under the Legal Services Authorities Act 1987, women, children, undertrials, SC/ST citizens, and individuals earning below prescribed state income limits (typically ₹3 Lakhs annually) are entitled to free legal counsel paid by the State. Visit your local District Court's DLSA front office to apply.
