# Indian Legal Fundamentals Glossary

Canonical terminology for everyday Indian citizens navigating police encounters, contracts, consumer disputes, courts, and cyber safety.

## Criminal Procedure & Police Powers

**Cognizable Offence**:
A category of serious crimes (e.g. theft, grievous hurt, rape, murder) where police officers have statutory authority to arrest without a warrant and are legally mandated to register an FIR immediately.
_Avoid_: Major crime, police case

**Non-Cognizable Offence**:
A category of less severe offences (e.g. simple assault, minor public nuisance, cheating without forgery) where police cannot arrest without a Magistrate's warrant and can only log an NCR (Non-Cognizable Report).
_Avoid_: Minor incident, petty quarrel

**FIR (First Information Report)**:
The initial written document recorded by police under Section 154 CrPC (Sec 173 BNSS) upon receiving information regarding the commission of a cognizable offence, setting criminal investigation into motion.
_Avoid_: Police complaint, incident diary

**Zero FIR**:
An FIR that can be registered at any police station across India irrespective of where the crime occurred or territorial jurisdiction, given a temporary serial number "0" and immediately transferred to the jurisdictional station.
_Avoid_: Universal report, emergency complaint

**Bailable Offence**:
An offence classified under the Code of Criminal Procedure where the accused has an absolute statutory right to be released on bail by the police station officer or court upon providing personal bond or surety.
_Avoid_: Easy case, minor bail

**Non-Bailable Offence**:
An offence where release on bail is not a matter of right for the accused, but rests within the sound judicial discretion of the Magistrate or Sessions Court based on gravity and flight risk.
_Avoid_: Jail-only crime, no-release case

**D.K. Basu Guidelines**:
The 11 mandatory procedural safeguards established by the Supreme Court in *D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal (1997)* governing police arrest, requiring name badges, a signed arrest memo with a witness, mandatory notification to relatives within 8-12 hours, and bi-daily medical examinations.
_Avoid_: Police rules, arrest handbook

## Track 1: Cyber Law, Banking & Digital Privacy

**Digital Arrest Scam**:
A cyber extortion scheme where fraudsters impersonate CBI, Police, ED, or Customs officers over Skype/WhatsApp video calls, falsely claiming the victim is implicated in narcotics or money laundering to coerce panic bank transfers into mule accounts.
_Avoid_: Fake police call, video fraud

**Zero Liability (RBI 2017)**:
The binding statutory principle under the Reserve Bank of India Master Circular (July 2017) establishing that a bank customer has zero financial liability for unauthorized electronic debit transactions if notified to the bank within three working days.
_Avoid_: Bank refund guarantee, loss cover

**Section 66D IT Act (Cheating by Personation)**:
The penal provision of the Information Technology Act 2000 punishing anyone who cheats by impersonating another person or agency using a computer device or network with up to three years imprisonment and a fine up to ₹1 Lakh.
_Avoid_: Online fraud rule, cyber trick penalty

**Data Principal**:
The individual to whom personal data relates under Section 2(j) of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, possessing statutory rights to consent withdrawal, data correction, and data erasure.
_Avoid_: Data user, internet user

**Data Fiduciary**:
Any person, corporation, or agency that determines the purpose and means of processing personal data under the DPDP Act 2023, subject to statutory security safeguards and penalties up to ₹250 Crores for data breaches.
_Avoid_: Tech company, data collector

**Intermediary Liability (Section 79 IT Act)**:
The statutory safe-harbour immunity protecting internet platforms from liability for third-party user content, conditioned on strict compliance with government takedown orders and judicial decrees (*Shreya Singhal v. Union of India*).
_Avoid_: Platform shield, app immunity

## Civil Law & Everyday Agreements

**Leave and License Agreement**:
A temporary contractual permission granted by a property owner (licensor) to a licensee to occupy and use premises for a specified duration (typically 11 months), without creating any tenancy rights or interest in the property.
_Avoid_: Rent lease, rental bond

**Consideration**:
The price or value bargained for and exchanged between contracting parties (money, goods, services, or forbearance) required under Section 2(d) of the Indian Contract Act 1872 to make a promise legally enforceable.
_Avoid_: Payment, price tag

**Section 138 NI Act (Cheque Bounce)**:
The statutory quasi-criminal provision under the Negotiable Instruments Act 1881 punishing the drawer of a dishonoured cheque with up to two years imprisonment and double the cheque amount if not paid within 15 days of receiving a statutory demand notice.
_Avoid_: Bad cheque crime, bank bounce penalty

**Legal Notice**:
A formal written communication sent via Registered Post or Speed Post giving formal notice of a grievance, specifying the legal basis, and providing a fixed deadline (typically 15 to 30 days) to resolve the issue before filing a court petition.
_Avoid_: Lawyer warning, court threat

**Deficiency in Service**:
Any fault, imperfection, shortcoming, or inadequacy in quality, nature, or manner of performance required to be maintained under law or contract by a service provider under Section 2(11) of the Consumer Protection Act 2019.
_Avoid_: Bad service, company cheating

## Fundamental Constitutional Rights & Latin Maxims

**Article 21**:
The paramount constitutional guarantee that no person shall be deprived of their life or personal liberty except according to fair, just, and reasonable procedure established by law (*Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India*).
_Avoid_: Freedom rule, life right

**Habeas Corpus**:
The constitutional prerogative writ issued by the Supreme Court (Article 32) or High Courts (Article 226) commanding detaining authorities to produce an incarcerated individual before the bench to examine the lawfulness of detention.
_Avoid_: Jail release writ, prisoner order

**Mens Rea**:
"Guilty mind"; the criminal mental state (intention, knowledge, or gross criminal recklessness) required by law to establish criminal liability (*Jacob Mathew v. State of Punjab*).
_Avoid_: Evil intent, bad thought

**Prima Facie**:
"At first face"; evidence sufficient to raise a presumption of law or establish a claim unless rebutted and contradicted by opposing evidence.

**Pro Se / Party-in-Person**:
A litigant who chooses to argue and present their case directly before a Magistrate, Consumer Commission, or High Court without the representation of an advocate.

**Res Ipsa Loquitur**:
"The thing speaks for itself"; the legal doctrine that negligence can be inferred from the very nature of an accident without direct proof of negligent acts (*IMA v. V.P. Shantha*).

**Nemo Tenetur Seipsum Accusare**:
"No man is bound to accuse himself"; the Latin maxim forming the core of Article 20(3) right to silence against self-incrimination.

**Caveat Emptor**:
"Let the buyer beware"; the traditional principle that buyers bear the risk of defects, significantly curtailed in modern Indian jurisprudence by the Consumer Protection Act 2019's statutory product liability.

**Bona Fide**:
In good faith; acting honestly, openly, and without any intention to deceive, mislead, or commit fraud.

**Quid Pro Quo**:
"Something for something"; a mutual reciprocal exchange where one party performs or provides something in explicit exchange for another benefit.

**Injunction**:
An authoritative judicial order issued under Section 38 of the Specific Relief Act commanding a defendant to refrain from doing a specific harmful act (temporary or permanent).
