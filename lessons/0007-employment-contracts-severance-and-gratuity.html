Segment 2 • Statutory Protection

The Gratuity Shield: Continuous Service & Formula

The Payment of Gratuity Act 1972 applies to every establishment employing 10 or more persons. Under Section 2A, completing 240 days in the 5th year (190 days for 5-day workweeks) satisfies the 5-year continuous service threshold.

The 240/190-Day Rule

Under Section 2A(2), an employee who has worked 4 years and 240 days (or 190 days in a 5-day corporate office) has legally satisfied the 5-year continuous service threshold.

Mandatory Statutory Formula

Gratuity = (15 × Last Drawn Basic + DA × Completed Years) ÷ 26. Tax-free up to ₹20 Lakhs under Section 10(10) of the Income Tax Act.

Mandatory 30-Day Payout

Under Section 7(3), the employer must disburse gratuity within 30 days of exit. Delayed payout attracts mandatory 10% simple interest p.a. under Section 7(3A).

Segment 4 • Case Example

Section 12 Maternity Shield: Absolute Dismissal Immunity

Under Section 12 of the Maternity Benefit Act 1961 (Amended 2017), there is an absolute statutory ban on discharging or dismissing a woman employee at any time during pregnancy or maternity leave.

Section 12 Absolute Bar

Any dismissal, discharge, or adverse notice issued during pregnancy or maternity leave is void ab initio. Corporate restructuring or PIP excuses cannot override Section 12.

26 Weeks Paid Leave

Guarantees 26 weeks of fully paid leave for up to two surviving children. Employers must pay average daily wage for the period of actual absence.

Criminal Penalties

Under Section 21, employers who discharge pregnant workers face mandatory imprisonment of up to 1 year and statutory fines, enforceable via the Labour Court.

Segment 1: The Sudden Termination Dilemma

It is four o'clock on a Friday afternoon. Your manager schedules an unexpected meeting. Without warning, you are handed a termination letter, your corporate laptop is seized, and your email access is revoked within minutes. The HR representative casually tells you that due to corporate restructuring, your three-month notice period will be waived without pay, your final two months of earned salary will be withheld indefinitely pending financial audits, and your five years of earned gratuity will be forfeited. In corporate offices, startups, and factories across India, thousands of professionals face this exact power imbalance every month. Most employees believe they are at the total mercy of corporate employment contracts. But under Indian labor and employment jurisprudence, statutory rights override private contractual waivers. In this lesson, you will master your statutory rights on termination, the mandatory five-year gratuity calculation rules, legal protections against wrongful deduction, and the absolute statutory shield protecting pregnant employees under the Maternity Benefit Act.

Segment 2: Gratuity Shield & 5-Year Continuous Service

The Payment of Gratuity Act of 1972 is a non-negotiable statutory benefit created to reward long-term service. Every establishment employing ten or more persons is legally bound by this statute. An employee becomes eligible for gratuity upon completing five years of continuous service. However, under Section 2A of the Act, continuous service does not require 365 days of physical work each year. In an establishment operating on a six-day workweek, completing two hundred and forty days of actual work in the fifth year satisfies the statutory test. For five-day corporate workweeks, just one hundred and ninety days in the fifth year legally qualifies you as having completed that full year. Gratuity is calculated using a mandatory formula: fifteen days of your last drawn basic salary plus dearness allowance, multiplied by your total completed years of service, divided by twenty-six. Under Section 7(3), the employer must disburse your gratuity within thirty days of termination. If the employer delays, Section 7(3A) mandates simple interest at ten percent per annum until the full sum is paid. Forfeiture is permitted only under narrow circumstances of proven riotous or violent conduct leading to financial damage, and only to the extent of actual damage caused.

Segment 3: Full & Final Settlement & Unlawful Deductions

When an employment relationship ends, the employer is legally obligated to issue your Full and Final settlement. Under state Shops and Commercial Establishments Acts and the Payment of Wages Act, all earned wages, accrued leave encashment, and statutory bonuses must be settled within seven to thirty days following the employee's last working day. Employers frequently attempt to deduct arbitrary penalties for alleged training costs, unserved notice periods during employer-initiated layoffs, or unreturned non-material assets. The Supreme Court established in Central Inland Water Transport Corporation that arbitrary, one-sided clauses imposed by employers through unequal bargaining power are unconscionable and void under Section 23 of the Indian Contract Act. If an employer terminates you immediately without cause, they must pay notice pay in lieu of the contractual notice period. Withholding earned wages is an actionable civil debt and an unfair labor practice. An employer cannot convert your terminal dues into hostage capital.

Segment 4: Maternity Protection Under Section 12

Consider Priya, a senior project lead at a software firm in Pune. When she informed HR that she was expecting a child and submitted her maternity leave application, the company placed her on a sudden performance improvement plan and issued a termination letter three weeks later, citing redundancy. Under Section 12 of the Maternity Benefit Act of 1961, as amended in 2017, this termination is completely illegal and void ab initio. Section 12 explicitly prohibits an employer from discharging or dismissing a woman worker at any time during her pregnancy or during her absence on maternity leave. Any termination during this period constitutes an offence punishable by imprisonment and hefty fines. Furthermore, the amended Act guarantees twenty-six weeks of fully paid maternity leave for up to two surviving children, mandatory creche facilities for establishments with fifty or more employees, and protection of all terminal benefits. When Priya served a formal statutory notice citing Section 12, the firm was compelled to withdraw the termination, reinstate her salary, and provide full paid maternity benefits.

Segment 5: The 4-Step Terminal Dues Recovery Playbook

If an employer unlawfully terminates your employment, withholds your terminal wages, or refuses to pay gratuity, execute this four-step legal playbook. Step One: Document and preserve all employment evidence. Download your payslips, appointment letter, official appraisal ratings, resignation or termination emails, and Form 26AS tax statements before corporate access is disabled. Step Two: Issue a Formal Statutory Demand Notice. Send a registered demand letter under the Payment of Gratuity Act and state Shops and Establishments Act demanding release of all pending salary, leave encashment, and gratuity with ten percent interest within fifteen days. Step Three: File Form I under the Gratuity Rules before the Controlling Authority. If gratuity is withheld past thirty days, submit Form I directly to the jurisdictional Labour Commissioner acting as the Controlling Authority. The authority has judicial powers to issue recovery certificates enforced as land revenue arrears. Step Four: Escalate wage violations to the Labour Court or SAMADHAN portal. Unpaid earned salaries can be reported online through the Ministry of Labour SAMADHAN grievance system, leading to conciliation proceedings and recovery orders.

Statutory Authorities & Landmark Precedents

  • Payment of Gratuity Act 1972: Section 2A (Continuous Service) & Section 7 (Disbursement within 30 days & 10% Interest).
  • Maternity Benefit (Amendment) Act 2017: Section 12 (Absolute Bar on Dismissal During Pregnancy).
  • Supreme Court of India: Central Inland Water Transport Corp v. Brojo Nath Ganguly (1986 3 SCC 156) — Unconscionable and arbitrary termination clauses are void under Section 23 of the Contract Act.
  • Ministry of Labour & Employment: SAMADHAN Industrial Dispute Portal.
