Indian Legal Fundamentals Glossary

Tight, plain-language definitions of statutory concepts, police powers, contract doctrines, and Latin maxims with authentic spoken pronunciations.

Criminal Procedure & Police Powers

Cognizable Offence

A serious crime where police have statutory power to arrest without a warrant and are legally mandated to register an FIR under Section 154 CrPC (*Lalita Kumari*).

Avoid: Major crime, police case
Non-Cognizable Offence

A minor offence where police cannot arrest without a Magistrate's warrant and records an NCR (Non-Cognizable Report).

Avoid: Petty quarrel, minor incident
FIR (First Information Report)

The earliest written record prepared by police upon receiving information of a cognizable offence, triggering criminal investigation.

Avoid: Police complaint, incident report
Zero FIR

An FIR that can be registered at any police station across India irrespective of territorial jurisdiction, assigned number "0", and transferred to the jurisdictional thana.

Avoid: Universal report, emergency report
D.K. Basu Guidelines

11 mandatory procedural safeguards established by the Supreme Court in 1997 governing police arrest, memo of arrest, medical checks, and relative notification.

Avoid: Police handbook, arrest guide

Track 1: Cyber Law, Banking & Digital Privacy

Digital Arrest Scam

A cyber extortion scheme where fraudsters impersonate CBI, Police, or Customs officers over Skype/WhatsApp video calls, falsely claiming the victim is implicated in narcotics or money laundering to coerce panic transfers into mule accounts.

Avoid: Fake police call, video fraud
Zero Liability (RBI 2017)

The binding statutory principle under the Reserve Bank of India Master Circular (July 2017) establishing that a bank customer has zero financial liability for unauthorized electronic debit transactions if notified to the bank within three working days.

Avoid: Bank refund guarantee, loss cover
Section 66D IT Act (Cheating by Personation)

The penal provision of the Information Technology Act 2000 punishing anyone who cheats by impersonating another person or agency using a computer device or network with up to three years imprisonment and a fine up to ₹1 Lakh.

Avoid: Online fraud rule, cyber trick penalty
Data Principal

The individual to whom personal data relates under Section 2(j) of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, possessing statutory rights to consent withdrawal, data correction, and data erasure.

Avoid: Data user, internet user
Data Fiduciary

Any person, corporation, or agency that determines the purpose and means of processing personal data under the DPDP Act 2023, subject to statutory security safeguards and penalties up to ₹250 Crores for data breaches.

Avoid: Tech company, data collector

Civil Law, Contracts & Consumer Protection

Leave and License Agreement

A temporary contractual permission granted by a property owner to a licensee to occupy premises for a fixed term (11 months) without creating tenancy rights under Section 52 of the Easements Act.

Avoid: Rental lease, rent agreement
Section 138 NI Act (Cheque Bounce)

Quasi-criminal provision holding the drawer of a dishonoured cheque liable for up to 2 years imprisonment and double the cheque fine if unpaid 15 days after statutory notice.

Avoid: Bad cheque rule, bank bounce fee
Deficiency in Service

Any fault, imperfection, shortcoming, or inadequacy in quality, nature, or manner of performance required under law or contract by a service provider under Section 2(11) of the Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Avoid: Bad service, company cheating

Constitutional Maxims & Latin Doctrines (with Spoken Audio)

Mens Rea

"Guilty mind" — the culpable criminal mental intent required by law to establish criminal liability (*Jacob Mathew v. State of Punjab*).

Avoid: Evil intent, bad thought
Prima Facie

"At first face" — evidence sufficient to disclose an offence or civil claim on its face, triggering mandatory FIR registration under *Lalita Kumari*.

Avoid: Surface proof, obvious look
Consensus Ad Idem

"Meeting of the minds" — common consent where both contracting parties agree upon the exact same thing in the exact same sense (Section 10, Contract Act).

Avoid: Shared promise, two-way deal
Habeas Corpus

"You have the body" — the constitutional prerogative writ under Article 32 or Article 226 commanding police to produce an unlawfully detained person before the bench.

Avoid: Jail release writ, prisoner order
Res Ipsa Loquitur

"The thing speaks for itself" — negligence is presumed from the very occurrence of an accident or medical surgical blunder without direct witness testimony.

Avoid: Obvious blunder, self-fault
Nemo Tenetur Seipsum Accusare

"No man is bound to accuse himself" — the foundational maxim underlying Article 20(3) right against self-incrimination during interrogation (*Nandini Satpathy*).

Avoid: Self-blame rule, confession shield
Modus Operandi

"Mode of operation" — the characteristic pattern, method, or signature behavior used by perpetrators to commit cyber frauds, impersonation, or criminal extortion.

Avoid: Crime method, scam habit
Ultra Vires

"Beyond the powers" — acts, regulations, or executive surveillance carried out beyond statutory or constitutional authority under Article 21 (*Puttaswamy*).

Avoid: Illegal rule, out of bound
Caveat Emptor

"Let the buyer beware" — substantially superseded in India by Chapter VI statutory strict product liability under the Consumer Protection Act 2019.

Avoid: Buyer risk, purchase warning
Quantum Meruit

"As much as he deserved" — legal doctrine under Section 70 of the Contract Act guaranteeing reasonable compensation for services actually rendered when an employment or service contract is terminated prematurely.

Avoid: Fair wage estimate, partial pay
In Camera

"In private chambers" — judicial and POSH Internal Committee proceedings held in strict privacy where the public, media, and unauthorized spectators are excluded under Section 16.

Avoid: Secret trial, hidden room meeting
Pro Se / Party-in-Person

Appearing and arguing your own matter directly before an Indian Magistrate, District Court, or Consumer Commission without hiring an advocate.

Avoid: Self-lawyering, solo defense
Suo Motu

"On its own motion" — action taken by an Information Commission, High Court, or public authority independently without requiring a formal petition, notably Section 4 proactive public disclosures under the RTI Act 2005.

Avoid: Self-starting case, random audit
Audi Alteram Partem

"Listen to the other side" — the cardinal rule of natural justice requiring Information Commissions and tribunals to grant a fair hearing to both the citizen and the Public Information Officer before dismissing an appeal or imposing Section 20 penalties.

Avoid: Two-way debate, informal talk
In Forma Pauperis

"In the character of an indigent person" — statutory exemption from application fees, court fees, and photocopying charges under Section 7(5) RTI Act for BPL cardholders and Order 33 CPC for citizens accessing free legal aid under NALSA.

Avoid: Poverty discount, concession request
Interest Reipublicae Ut Sit Finis Litium

"It is in the public interest that there should be an end to litigation" — the foundational judicial doctrine governing Lok Adalats under Section 21 of the Legal Services Authorities Act, declaring consent awards final, binding as civil court decrees, and non-appealable.

Avoid: Compromise rule, case closure treaty
