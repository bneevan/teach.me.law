Guiding Purpose & Philosophy

Course Mission: Everyday Indian Citizen Empowerment

Demystify the Indian legal system so everyday citizens can confidently protect their fundamental constitutional rights, recognize binding contracts, enforce consumer protections, defend against cyber scams, and interact lawfully with police without fear or extortion.

Success Looks Like: Demonstrated Citizen Outcomes

1. The Legal Map & Jurisdiction Confidently distinguish between civil disputes and criminal charges in India, understanding cognizable vs. non-cognizable offenses, bailable vs. non-bailable warrants, and court hierarchies (*Jacob Mathew* and *Lalita Kumari*).
2. Everyday Contracts & Tenancy Review contracts, job agreements, and 11-month Leave & License rent agreements under the Indian Contract Act 1872 and IT Act 2000, spotting illegal penalty clauses (*Fateh Chand*) and unconscionable terms (*Brojo Nath Ganguly*).
3. Consumer Protection & e-Daakhil Enforce consumer rights under the Consumer Protection Act 2019 via e-Daakhil, drafting formal Legal Notices and claiming compensation for deficiency in service and mental agony (*LDA v. M.K. Gupta*, *IMA v. V.P. Shantha*).
4. Police Encounters & Constitutional Shield Exercise constitutional safeguards during police interactions, searches, traffic stops, and arrest using the Supreme Court's landmark *D.K. Basu* guidelines, *Arnesh Kumar* Section 41A notice safeguards, and Article 20(3) right to silence (*Nandini Satpathy*).
5. Cyber Frauds & Digital Privacy Defend against modern cyber frauds, UPI phishing, and fake "digital arrest" extortion using the RBI Zero Liability Master Circular, the National Cybercrime Helpline 1930, and the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023.
6. Workplace Safety & Terminal Protections Enforce the Payment of Gratuity Act 1972 continuous service thresholds (240/190-day rule), recover withheld Full & Final dues with 10% statutory interest, exercise absolute pregnancy dismissal immunity under Section 12 Maternity Benefit Act, and uphold safe working environments under the POSH Act 2013 (*Vishaka*).
7. Public Remedies, RTI & Legal Aid (Track 3: Lessons 0009–0010) Enforce government transparency under the Right to Information (RTI Act 2005) with 48-hour life/liberty emergency filings and Section 20 PIO personal penalties (*Raj Narain*, *CBSE v. Aditya Bandopadhyay*), and secure 100% free institutional legal aid under NALSA (Article 39A / Section 12) and binding dispute resolution via Lok Adalats (*Hussainara Khatoon*).

Guiding Principles & Pedagogical Standards

Beginner-First Clarity Zero unexplained legalese; every concept is explained causally with plain-English expansions, Latin pronunciations, and real-world Indian examples.
Unconstrained Narrative Depth Lesson length is never cut short arbitrarily; scenarios and landmark Supreme Court verdicts are explored with full statutory context and practical nuances.
Dual-Mode Audio-First Delivery Spoken audio narration generated via local Kokoro TTS (`am_adam`) is 100% coherent eyes-free, while HTML visual cards and reference tables stand independently ears-free.
